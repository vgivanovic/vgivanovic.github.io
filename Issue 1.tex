\vspace{-1in}\textbf{\large{}LASD Matters, Issue 1: Transparency, Bonds, and Taxes}\newline

Hello! I'm Vladimir Ivanovic, a former Los Altos School District (LASD) Board
member. I resigned in March 2026 because I could not support the Board's
decision to move 650 Bullis Charter School (BCS) students to a new campus to be
built just for them on land that LASD acquired in 2019. I think it's the wrong
decision for a multiple reasons that I'll go over in another issue of ``LASD
Matters''. \newline

For now, I want to highlight what I believe is the Board's deliberate lack of
transparency about what a recent bond measure was for, and the why that's
important. \newline

In November 2024 LASD voters passed Measure EE, a \$350M bond measure to upgrade
LASD facilities. Strange as it may seem, the measure's legally required ballot
summary didn't mention that 40\% of the bond proceeds were to be used to give BCS
the newest and best campus in all of LASD. This opacity was not accidental
because being upfront about how 40\% of Measure EE was not going to benefit LASD
schools would have sunk the bond measure. As it turns out, the measure barely
passed with just a 1.2\% margin, leaving LASD taxpayers on the hook to pay back
an estimated \$730M over the next 30 years that won't do what they expected.
\newline

There are at least two important and serious consequences that have barely been
discussed in public. One is that Measure EE will increase property taxes by
\textasciitilde{}\$600 to \textasciitilde{}\$1200 or more per year until Measure EE is paid off -- in 30 years.
(The actual annual increase is \$30 per \$100,000 of assessed value. For a home
assessed at \$2M, that \$600/year; for a \$4M home, that \$1200/year.) The upsetting
part is that only a portion of this new property tax will benefit LASD schools.
\newline

Another consequence, not discussed at all, is the difficulty LASD will face to
pass two or more additional bond measures, bond measures that must be passed to
complete the Facilities Master Plan. People, in general, don't like to be
hoodwinked twice, so it's doubtful that these new bond measures will pass.
Additionally, the pass rate of bond measures in LASD has been dropping for
years, and that will also make passing two more bond measures hard. The most
likely outcome, once again, is that LASD will not have enough money to upgrade
its existing facilities nor replace portables with permanent buildings, leaving
BCS with LASD's only modern campus. I don't think this is an outcome that voters
expected or wanted. \newline
